Musikalisches Würfelspiel · K. 516f

Musical Dice Game

A musical dice game commonly attributed to Mozart. Roll two dice for each of sixteen bars, look each total up in the published table, and hear the minuet that falls out.

    How it works

    The game, published by Nikolaus Simrock in 1793 under Mozart's name, supplies 176 numbered one-bar pieces of music and a table with eleven rows (dice totals 2 to 12) and sixteen columns (the bars of a minuet). For each bar you throw two dice and play the numbered measure the table names. Every row of a column fits the same place in the same harmony, so any throw yields a coherent minuet in C major.

    This page does exactly that. Two independent fair dice are simulated for every bar and their faces are kept, not just the total, so seven comes up far more often than two or twelve, just as at a table. The URL records those dice and your locks; open it anywhere and the same minuet is rebuilt. Bar 8 has one printed version with a first and a second ending, and bar 16 two; the other fourteen bars each have eleven, which gives 1114 × 2 selection sequences. Many of those sequences contain the same music, since several table entries are identical measures, so that number is not a count of distinct pieces.

    The sound is made in your browser from the notes of the public-domain score, with a plain struck-string tone, and the WAV you download is the same rendering laid end to end. Nothing is fetched while you play. "Play with repeats" performs the minuet as printed: the first half twice, with the first ending the first time through bar 8, then the second half once.

    Whether Mozart wrote the game is unverified: his name is on the cover, and a manuscript of 176 bars in his hand from 1787 exists without instructions or dice, but the link between the two has not been established.
